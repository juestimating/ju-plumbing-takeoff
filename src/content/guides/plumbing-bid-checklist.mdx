A plumbing bid checklist catches the errors that survive a good estimate: an addendum you did not price, a trade boundary nobody settled, a bond form left blank, a total that does not match the bid form. Run it after the numbers are done and before anything leaves your office, and have someone who did not build the estimate read the finished bid cold. Bid rules differ by owner and jurisdiction, so the instructions to bidders control, and this list sits on top of them.

## Plumbing bid checklist: confirm the documents and addenda first

- **Sheet index against the sheets you hold.** A sheet you do not hold may be a system nobody priced. Check revision dates and clouds too.
- **Specifications and bid instructions.** Division 22 and the related sections, the general and supplementary conditions, the bid form and any list of required forms.
- **Every addendum, by number and date,** in a log of what changed, which sheets or sections it touched and whether the price moved.
- **Each addendum applied to the quantities.** A revised sheet means re-measuring the affected runs and fixtures, not relabeling the sheet.
- **Acknowledgment.** On public work a missing acknowledgment can make a bid non-responsive, so list the addenda the way the form asks. On a subcontract proposal, state the addenda you priced.
- **Pre-bid minutes and RFI responses.** Answers given at a pre-bid meeting or in an RFI log can change scope even when no addendum follows.
- **Due date, time, time zone and method,** written at the top of your checklist.

<Callout type="warn" title="Look for new addenda late">

Check for new addenda on the morning of bid day and again before your internal deadline. If one lands too late to price, read how the instructions handle late changes, and tell the general contractor what you did and did not include.

</Callout>

## Check scope: inclusions, exclusions and trade boundaries

Scope gaps are harder to see than math errors, because nothing in the spreadsheet flags them. For each Division 22 section, find the line in the estimate or the exclusion in the proposal, and check Divisions 21, 23, 31 and 33 where plumbing crews work, and Division 26 for who wires plumbing equipment.

Then take the [trade-split table in the plumbing takeoff checklist](/guides/plumbing-takeoff-checklist#testing-exclusions-and-trade-splits-to-write-down) row by row, from excavation and coring to backflow test reports, utility tap fees and temporary water. Each row needs a line in your price or a written exclusion in your proposal. Give underground scope its own pass, covering the excavation split, depth bands and connections.

### Alternates, unit prices and allowances

- **Alternates.** Price every alternate on the form as an add or deduct as stated, check whether it changes the base bid (deleted fixtures, a changed pipe material), and never leave one blank.
- **Unit prices.** Price every unit requested, in the unit the form names (per LF, EA or CY), consistent with your base bid, because owners use them to price changes.
- **Allowances.** Cash allowances go in at the stated amount. Note how unspent amounts are handled.
- **Substitutions.** Check the approved manufacturers and the or-equal language, and if you priced an equal, how and when it must be submitted.

## Check the numbers behind the price

A clean bid form on top of a takeoff with a missing riser is still the wrong number. Before you trust the total, confirm that:

- fixtures reconcile to the schedule and the plans, and every riser was followed from the underground connection to the roof
- each supplier and subcontractor quote acknowledges the addenda you priced and leaves out nothing you assumed it included
- markups follow your rules in a fixed order on a fixed base, and you know whether each is taken on cost or as a margin on price
- the summary equals the tabs, the bid form equals the summary, and alternates and unit prices tie back to their sources
- the total makes sense against your history by system and by fixture count or floor area, and any gap has an explanation

The pricing method is in [how to estimate a plumbing job](/guides/how-to-estimate-a-plumbing-job), and [plumbing estimating mistakes](/guides/plumbing-estimating-mistakes) lists the misses that cost bids.

## Check bonds, insurance, taxes and registration paperwork

| Paperwork | What to check |
|---|---|
| Bid security | The bond or other security on the form the instructions name, the amount, signatures and seal, and the surety's power of attorney attached |
| Bonding for award | Capacity confirmed with your surety early, a consent of surety if required, and whether the performance and payment bond premium is in your price |
| Insurance | Limits, additional insured wording and waivers against your certificate, and on a wrap-up program (OCIP or CCIP), how enrollment works and what you may deduct |
| Taxes | Sales or use tax on material, and any exemption that applies to this owner or project type |
| Registrations | License numbers where the jurisdiction requires them, business registration, a W-9, any prequalification package, and local or state forms |
| Public work | Prevailing wage requirements, small and disadvantaged business participation forms with documented good-faith efforts, domestic-content rules on federally funded work, and sworn statements such as non-collusion affidavits |

Brokers and sureties need days, not hours. Ask for documents when you start the estimate, not when you finish it.

## Check the bid form, validity period and schedule

- **Every line completed,** with items you are not pricing marked the way the instructions say.
- **Numbers and words** match where the form asks for both. The instructions say which controls if they differ.
- **Signature.** An authorized person, in the right capacity, with any seal, notarization or witness the form requires.
- **Corrections** initialed only if the instructions allow it. Otherwise, print a clean form.
- **Qualifications.** Public bid forms usually do not allow conditions or exclusions written on them, and a bid with its own conditions can be rejected as non-responsive. A subcontract proposal to a general contractor is different: a scope letter with inclusions, exclusions and clarifications is normal. Know which one you are writing.
- **Validity period.** Confirm your supplier quotes and labor rates hold for as many days as the bid must stay open, and add escalation if the award or start may slip.
- **Schedule and terms.** Milestones, completion dates, liquidated damages, phasing, working hours, payment terms, retainage and pay-when-paid language. Price what you can, qualify what you may, and ask about the rest before bid day.

## Bid-day habits that prevent late mistakes

- **An internal deadline,** well ahead of the official one, and a time after which only one person can change the bid.
- **One owner for the final numbers.** Two people editing the same bid is how a total and a bid form stop agreeing.
- **The right clock:** the time zone and time source in the instructions, because portals and email servers keep their own.
- **Early submission.** Large files and upload portals fail near the deadline. Keep the confirmation or receipt, and phone to confirm if the method gives none.
- **Written confirmation.** If a general contractor calls on bid day for a final number, repeat it by email with the inclusions and the addenda it covers.
- **A frozen copy** of exactly what you sent, as a PDF of the forms and the final workbook, never edited afterwards. The [plumbing estimating spreadsheet](/guides/plumbing-estimating-spreadsheet) guide covers version control.
- **A record of the result,** with the reason where you know it, so your history has one more data point.
